Keyboard shortcuts
==================

*Every key and gesture of EMStudio, grouped by where it acts.*

The same list is in the application under ``Help ▸ Shortcuts``, where you can
search it. The tables below are drawn from the same source, so they say what the
application does.

Where a key acts
----------------

A key acts on the part of the window that has the focus, and the tables are
grouped the same way.

- **Everywhere**: the keys of the whole application, such as saving, the
  settings, the chip's menu at **Where you work** and adding a node.
- **On the graph**: the keys of a Graph window (the matrix, the free graph, the
  DTC) and of the Outliner. They act on the canvas and on the Outliner, and leave
  a field you are typing in, or a focused button, alone.
- **In the Space**: the keys of the 3D scene when it has the focus. They follow
  the conventions of Blender, so that whoever models in Blender finds the same
  hands here: :kbd:`G`, :kbd:`R` and :kbd:`S` move, rotate and scale, the numeric
  keypad chooses the views, :kbd:`⇧A` adds a shape at the 3D cursor.
- **Tracing in the document**: the keys of the Viewer while you trace a region
  on an image.
- **Windows and spaces**: the gestures that arrange the windows and the
  workspaces.
- **In a field** and **in menus and buttons**: the keys of typing and of moving
  through a menu.

The same key can mean different things in different places: :kbd:`⇧A` adds a
node to the graph when the pointer is on the matrix, and a shape to the scene
when the 3D has the focus.

Keys and gestures
-----------------

The keys are given as the Mac shows them and as Windows and Linux show them: ⌘
(Command) becomes :kbd:`Ctrl`, ⌥ (Option) becomes :kbd:`Alt`, ⇧ is :kbd:`Shift`.
A gesture (a click, a drag, the wheel) is described in words and is the same on
every platform; where its description names ⌘ or ⌥, read :kbd:`Ctrl` or
:kbd:`Alt` on Windows and Linux.

On the web the browser keeps some keys for itself, for example the one that
closes the tab: the last table, *In the menus*, says which keys the menus show
and which of them the web leaves to the browser.
